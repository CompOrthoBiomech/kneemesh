kneemesh.cartilage.classify_sides(surface, bone_distance_field, side_cosine=0.3)[source]¶

Split a closed cartilage surface into its articular and bone sides.

Only the largest connected piece of the surface is used, so small islands of the segmentation are dropped. Each point’s outward normal is compared with the gradient of the bone’s distance field there, which points away from the bone wherever the bone is. kneemesh.segmentation.assemble_label_maps(label_maps, interpolator='label_gaussian', padding=4)[source]¶

Resample single-label LabelMaps onto one grid and combine them into a multilabel Segmentation.

Every nonzero voxel of an input belongs to its structure. The shared grid takes the first input’s direction cosines, the smallest voxel spacing of any input along each axis, and the bounding box of all inputs in physical space, padded with background on every side. Input axes count toward the shared axis nearest them in direction, and the grid’s voxel faces line up with the input that has the smallest spacing along each axis. Structures get label values 1, 2, … in the order given. Where resampled structures overlap, the one given later wins.

Parameters:

  • label_maps (Mapping[str, Image]) – Single-label 3D LabelMaps by structure name.

  • interpolator (Interpolator) – “label_gaussian” resamples with sitkLabelGaussian, which smooths the voxel staircase on finer grids. “nearest_neighbor” uses sitkNearestNeighbor.

  • padding (int) – Background voxels added beyond the bounding box on each side.

Returns:

The assembled segmentation, sitkUInt8 for up to 255 structures and sitkUInt16 above that.

Raises:

ValueError – If label_maps is empty, an input is not a 3D LabelMap of an accepted pixel type or has no foreground, interpolator is unknown, padding is negative, or a structure vanishes in resampling or is entirely covered by later ones.

Return type:

Segmentation

kneemesh.mesh_sets.surface_sets(elements)[source]¶

Return the “surface” element and node sets of a surface mesh: every element and every node it uses.

Parameters:

elements (Mapping[str, ndarray])

Return type:

tuple[dict[str, ndarray], dict[str, ndarray]]

Node optimization of hexahedral meshes for the scaled Jacobian and the Frobenius aspect ratio.

Up to three strategies run in turn on the hexahedra’s corner nodes:

  1. Smart Laplacian smoothing moves each node toward the average of its edge neighbors, keeping a move only if it does not lower the worst scaled Jacobian of the elements around the node.

  2. If any element is still inverted, untangling minimizes the sum of max(0, 0.2 - SJ)^2 over every element corner, which stays finite and smooth through inverted corners, until none is inverted.

  3. Optimization minimizes the sum of 1 / SJ over every element corner, plus aspect_weight times the sum of each corner’s squared Frobenius aspect ratio, by projected gradient descent. Both terms grow without bound as a corner degenerates, so no step can invert an element, and both weigh the worst corners most. 1 / SJ alone squares corners up by stretching elements; the aspect term holds their edge lengths in proportion. A third term, warp_weight times the sum over the boundary quadrilaterals of their squared normalized out-of-plane volume, flattens warped faces on the surface, which corner measures cannot see.

High-order hexahedra are also measured at the points of the Gauss rules simulations integrate them with, where their curved edges can invert an element whose corners are valid. Their Jacobians there are barriers in optimization and targets in untangling, alongside the corners.

Boundary nodes are classified from the mesh’s own boundary quadrilaterals. Where adjacent boundary faces meet at more than a feature angle, their shared edges are feature edges. Surface nodes slide in their tangent plane and may leave it by a fraction of the local element size; nodes on a feature edge slide along it with the same allowance off it; feature corners stay fixed. The inner nodes of a mesh’s straight_lines stay at their fractions along the segment between the line’s ends, which move as their own kind allows. By default the surface nodes’ net offset off their tangent planes is removed, which keeps the mesh’s volume. Tangential travel is also limited, so the tangent plane stays a good model of the surface. High-order nodes follow the corners of their element.

kneemesh.mesh_optimization.optimize_hexahedral_mesh(mesh, laplacian_iterations=10, optimization_iterations=200, surface_deviation=0.1, surface_travel=0.5, feature_angle=30.0, preserve_volume=True, aspect_weight=0.0, warp_weight=0.0)[source]¶

Move a hexahedral mesh’s nodes to raise its scaled Jacobians, keeping connectivity.

Smart Laplacian smoothing runs first, then optimization of the sum of 1 / SJ over every element corner plus aspect_weight times the sum of their squared Frobenius aspect ratios, plus warp_weight times the sum of the boundary quadrilaterals’ squared warp measures (_warp_objective). Optimization needs every element valid, so if smoothing leaves any corner’s scaled Jacobian below 0.01, inverted or nearly, the mesh is untangled first, by raising every corner’s toward 0.2 until all reach 0.01, and optimization is skipped with a warning if any stays inverted.

High-order hexahedra are also measured at their Gauss points (see scaled_jacobians), where their curved edges can invert them though their corners are valid: smoothing keeps their scaled Jacobians there from falling, untangling raises them as it does the corners’, and optimization adds (1 / SJ - 1 / 0.2)^2 for each below 0.2, a barrier that leaves elements well shaped there alone.

Boundary allowances scale with each node’s local size, the mean length of the element edges at the node. Feature edges are boundary edges where the faces on either side meet at more than feature_angle; nodes where feature edges end, branch, or turn by more than feature_angle are fixed.

Parameters:

  • mesh (Mesh) – Mesh of linear or high-order hexahedra, such as hexahedral_mesh returns.

  • laplacian_iterations (int) – Smart Laplacian sweeps. 0 skips smoothing.

  • optimization_iterations (int) – Gradient steps of the optimization, and at most as many of untangling. 0 skips both.

  • surface_deviation (float) – How far a boundary node may move off its starting tangent plane, or off its feature edge, as a fraction of its local size. 0 keeps boundary nodes on their tangent planes and edge lines.

  • surface_travel (float) – How far a boundary node may slide along its tangent plane or feature edge, as a fraction of its local size.

  • feature_angle (float) – Angle in degrees between boundary face normals above which their shared edge is a feature edge.

  • preserve_volume (bool) – Remove the area-weighted mean of the surface nodes’ offsets off their tangent planes, so they can move out in some places and in at others but not swell or shrink the mesh overall. Without it, optimization tends to push the whole surface outward, adding about 5% to a meniscus at surface_deviation 0.1.

  • aspect_weight (float) – Weight of the squared Frobenius aspect ratios against 1 / SJ. 0 optimizes the scaled Jacobian alone, which can stretch elements: it raised the real medial tibial cartilage’s largest aspect ratio to 42 with its walls free to bend and 11 with them straight, against 6.4 at 0.5. The aspect term also penalizes deliberately elongated elements, such as the meniscus’s along its centerline.

  • warp_weight (float) – Weight of the boundary quadrilaterals’ warp against 1 / SJ, per quadrilateral. 0 leaves warped faces as they are.

Removing overclosure: where built structures’ geometry overlaps, moving it apart after every structure is built.

Every structure is built from its own label, and smoothing, thickening, and fitting let neighbors overlap by a few tenths of a millimeter: on the example knee the medial meniscus reaches 0.45 mm into its tibial cartilage, the cartilages 0.15 mm into their bones, and the femoral and tibial cartilages 0.1 mm into each other. resolve_overclosure moves them apart by a priority:

  • Cartilage is thin, so it never yields to a meniscus or bone. Where two cartilages overlap, both articular sides are lowered toward their bones, each by its share of the two local thicknesses, t_a / (t_a + t_b), and the sheets rebuilt (geo.sheet.lower_articular).

  • Every other structure, in priority order, is pushed out of the union of every structure ranked above it: a meniscus by moving its contour points out within their slice planes and lofting them again, which keeps its planar caps and both hexahedral designs, and a bone by moving its surface’s vertices.

Overlaps are measured against each B-rep’s outer boundary, triangulated finely (brep_boundary), and each bone’s surface.

Settings for removing overclosure: moving built structures apart where their geometry overlaps.

After every structure is built, each is moved out of the structures that rank above it in priority. Cartilage is thin, so it ranks above every meniscus and bone and never moves for them. kneemesh.geo.meniscus.slice_isocontour(mesh, curve, spacing, points_per_slice=32, clip_height=None, clip_ratio=0.0, groove_ratio=0.25)[source]¶

Slice a mesh with planes normal to a curve at uniform arc-length spacing.

Slices are spaced exactly spacing apart along the curve, with any remainder split equally between its two ends, and are ordered along the curve from u = 0. Each cut is resampled to ordered points with a closed Kochanek spline: the section’s corners, tracked through the slices by their turning, at the same point indices on every slice, and points evenly spaced between them (_follow_corners). Slices are removed from either end of the curve until one encloses at least 20% of the average area.

Where the curve bends more tightly than the section is wide, or kinks, planes normal to it would cut through their neighbors’ contours, and a loft through them folds. There the planes are tilted apart, their centers staying on the curve, until every contour lies at least a quarter of the spacing in front of the plane before it and behind the plane after it (_separate_planes).

In-plane axes are defined from the curve’s best-fit plane, so they do not twist along a roughly planar curve.

With clip_height or clip_ratio, each section’s thin inner edge, its sharpest corner, is cut off straight where the section first reaches that height, measured across the direction from its centroid to the corner (see _clip_inner_edges). The contour is then its curved part, from one corner of the cut to the other, and a straight run back.

Shallow concavities of each section, grooves the smoothed surface keeps from the label, are treated as noise and filled in toward the section’s convex hull before corners are tracked (_fill_grooves): fully up to groove_ratio of their width deep, fading out at twice that.

Parameters:

  • mesh (vtkPolyData) – Closed surface from extract_isocontour or smooth_isocontour.

  • curve (BSpline) – Curve from centerline.

  • spacing (float) – Arc-length distance between slices, in physical units.

  • points_per_slice (int) – Number of points on each contour.

  • clip_height (float | None) – Height in physical units at which to cut off the sections’ thin inner edges, or None to use clip_ratio.

  • clip_ratio (float) – Height at which to cut off the sections’ thin inner edges, as a fraction of the mean of the sections’ greatest heights, those of their outer sides. 0 keeps the inner edges.

  • groove_ratio (float) – Depth, as a fraction of its width, up to which a section’s concavity is filled in to the section’s convex hull, and partly filled up to twice that. 0 keeps every concavity.

Returns:

The retained ContourSlices.

Raises:

kneemesh.geo.meniscus.contours_to_hex_blocks(slices, spline_order=3)[source]¶

Build a planar-capped solid from contour slices as five blocks for hexahedral meshing.

The section is split with a butterfly (O-grid) partition: an inner quadrilateral surrounded by four blocks, one for each of four arcs of the boundary. Every boundary point meets a spoke running inward, so the cells along the boundary stay close to square even at its sharp corners. A partition that puts block corners at those corners instead gives cells there the corner’s angle, or nearly 180 degrees where smoothing rounds it.

The four split points divide each contour into arcs of equal length, rotated to keep them as far as possible from the sharpest turns averaged over all slices. Straight spokes run from each split point to a corner of the inner quadrilateral, partway toward the section’s centroid.

The blocks’ faces are B-spline surfaces interpolating the slices with a knot vector shared by every face along the curve, so neighboring faces meet exactly. contours_to_brep builds its surface the same way, but at shared contour parameters rather than split at the block corners, so the two differ slightly.

  • slices (list[ContourSlice]) – Contours from slice_isocontour, in order along the curve, all with the same number of points.

  • spline_order (int) – Polynomial degree of the section curves. The degree along the curve is at most 3.

Returns:

kneemesh.geo.sheet.fit_sheet(sides, perimeter_radius=3.0, perimeter_inset=0.0, min_thickness=0.2, hole_thickness=0.0, frame=None)[source]¶

Fit a cartilage sheet to the two sides of a cartilage surface.

By default the domain is the plane normal to the thinnest principal direction of the side points. Each side’s footprint is where its points project onto the domain, with gaps of a few point spacings closed, and the sheet covers where both footprints overlap, so the steep perimeter between the sides is cut off. The overlap is moved perimeter_inset inward and rounded by rolling a disk of radius perimeter_radius around it, inside and out: a morphological closing then opening, from exact distance maps. Its outline then curves no more tightly than that radius anywhere, concave or convex, which removes the jagged edge and the notches and spurs narrower than the elements that must follow it. A Gaussian of half that radius then smooths the outline’s remaining waviness. Holes in the overlap are filled unless they become holes.

Heights are interpolated linearly between each side’s points on a grid, smoothed lightly, and fitted with bicubic splines. Where the thickness falls below min_thickness of the mean, the bone side is lowered to that thickness by a smooth maximum, leaving the articular side alone.

The wall at the perimeter is tilted so it leaves both sides at about equal angles: where the sheet curves down at its rim, a wall along the height lines meets the articular side at up to 130 degrees or more. A straight wall cannot meet both sides of a tapering rim at right angles, so the tilt balances the two, limited to _MAX_WALL_TILT of the local thickness and _MAX_TILT_REACH of perimeter_radius, and smoothed along the perimeter. Its band fades out at perimeter_radius inside the perimeter.

Parameters:

  • sides (CartilageSides) – Points of the cartilage’s articular and bone sides from classify_sides.

  • perimeter_radius (float) – Smallest radius of curvature of the perimeter and hole outlines, in physical units. Keep it above about half the elements’ size along the perimeter, or elements across a tight notch can invert.

  • perimeter_inset (float) – How far inside the sides’ overlap to put the perimeter, in physical units, cutting off more of the thin rim.

  • min_thickness (float) – Minimum thickness as a fraction of the mean.

  • hole_thickness (float) – Thickness as a fraction of the mean below which the sheet gets a hole, smaller than min_thickness. Regions of the overlap with no cartilage become holes too. Only regions whose outlines lie at least perimeter_radius inside the perimeter become holes; others are thickened. Holes are rounded like the perimeter, so those narrower than perimeter_radius vanish. 0 makes no holes.

  • frame (SheetFrame | None)

Returns:

The fitted sheet.

kneemesh.geo.sheet.sheet_to_brep(sheet)[source]¶

Build a solid of a cartilage sheet: its two sides, the wall between them at the perimeter, and the walls of its holes.

Each side is one bicubic B-spline surface interpolating the side’s points over a grid of domain points, trimmed by the perimeter and the hole outlines drawn in its parameter space, so both sides share one parameterization: the surface parameters are the domain coordinates (s, t). Each wall is ruled between matching edges of the two sides, so it is straight from side to side, tilted at the perimeter as fit_sheet describes and along the height lines at the holes. Hexahedral meshing relies on both: mesh.sheet.hexahedral_mesh pairs the sides’ points at equal parameters and follows the walls’ straight lines.

Hexahedral meshing of cartilage sheets: a quadrilateral mesh of one side, extruded through the thickness.

A sheet’s solid (geo.sheet.sheet_to_brep) has two sides that share one parameterization and walls ruled straight between them. One side is meshed with quadrilaterals, and each node’s column runs straight to the other side: from interior nodes to the other side’s point at the same parameters, and from nodes on a rim along the wall’s straight line through them. The columns are divided into layers, so the elements stack through the thickness.

The quadrilaterals come from gmsh, by default from its quasi-structured algorithm, which aligns a cross field (a 4-RoSy field) with the boundary, places the field’s singularities where the shape needs them, and meshes along it, leaving few irregular nodes. The regions those few nodes’ straight lines of edges bound are a partition of the sheet into four-sided blocks, which sheet_partition_brep can build.

Both meshes of a sheet have the sets “articular_side” and “bone_side” besides “surface” (mesh_sets): the faces on the side away from the bone, the side above, and on the side against it.

Build the partition of a sheet’s solid into blocks that its hexahedral mesh’s irregular nodes define, one volume per block.

The side is meshed as hexahedral_mesh meshes it. The straight lines of edges through the quadrilaterals’ irregular nodes, interior nodes with other than four quadrilaterals and rim nodes with other than two, run until they reach a rim or another irregular node. They divide the quadrilaterals into four-sided patches of regular grids, the mesh’s base complex. Each patch becomes a block: a patch on each side through its nodes’ columns’ ends, and ruled walls along its columns. The blocks are fragmented so neighbors share faces, as for transfinite meshing.

The quasi-structured algorithm leaves few irregular nodes and so few blocks: 10 on the real lateral tibial cartilage and 32 on the femoral at 2 mm. Frontal-Delaunay quadrilaterals leave many, and most of their blocks would be single elements.

kneemesh.gui.worker.run_job(job, queue)[source]¶

Run a GUI job and report its log and result on queue.

A job is a dict whose kind is geometry, mesh, preview, or overclosure:

  • geometry: entries (LabelEntry dicts) composed into a segmentation with interpolator and padding, written to assembled_label_map if that is given, then each of structures built with the LabelMapBrep config and previewed. What each was built from is saved beside its preview, for overclosure jobs.

  • overclosure: the geometry files geometry ({structure: path}) moved apart where they overlap (overclosure.resolve_overclosure), with the overclosure section of the LabelMapBrep config. A cartilage or meniscus moves only if this session built the file, so what it was built from is saved; the rest stay in place. Moved files are rewritten in place and previewed; the result names only those.

  • mesh: the BrepMesh config, whose breps are meshed, each also written as a .vtu preview with its faces and quality metrics.

  • preview: each B-rep in breps ({structure: path}) previewed.

Previews go to preview_directory. The result maps each structure to the paths of its output and its preview.
