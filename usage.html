Usage¶

kneemesh-assemble combines single-label LabelMaps into one multilabel LabelMap. kneemesh-brep builds a B-rep of every structure in a segmentation, and kneemesh-mesh meshes each structure’s B-rep. Each takes a JSON configuration, --option value overrides, or both, and --help lists every option and its default. Every option is a field of the command’s model, LabelMapAssembly, LabelMapBrep, or BrepMesh, with underscores written as dashes. Settings for one kind of structure sit in a section, such as meniscus, and are given on the command line as --meniscus.points-per-slice 10. The two menisci share meniscus, and the tibial and patellar cartilage cartilage; any of them can have settings of its own instead, in a section named for it, such as lateral_meniscus. Its own section is layered over the shared one field by field, so --lateral-meniscus.spacing 2 changes only the lateral meniscus’s spacing and leaves its other settings, and the medial meniscus’s, as meniscus gives them. To clear a field in a structure’s own section that the shared one sets, such as inner_clip_ratio when giving inner_clip_height, set it to null there.

The structure names, keys of labels, label_maps, and breps, pick each structure’s pipeline: lateral_meniscus, medial_meniscus, patellar_cartilage, femoral_cartilage, lateral_tibial_cartilage, medial_tibial_cartilage, femur, tibia, and patella. All are implemented. The bones’ geometry is a triangulated surface rather than a B-rep; see Bones.

Each structure’s output is written to <output-directory>/<structure><output>, where --output is a suffix. B-rep suffixes end in .brep, .brep by default; bones’ surfaces take their own section’s --femur.output, --tibia.output, or --patella.output, .stl by default. Mesh suffixes default to _{element_type}.msh, with {element_type} replaced by --element-type: hex8, hex20, hex27, tet4 (the default), or tet10, or for a bone by its own --femur.element-type, --tibia.element-type, or --patella.element-type. .vtu and .vtkhdf meshes hold their node and element sets (see Node and element sets); gmsh infers other formats from the extension and writes the elements only.

kneemesh-brep --label-map meniscus.nii --labels '{"medial_meniscus": 1}' --meniscus.cap planar
kneemesh-brep --label-map knee.nii --labels '{"lateral_meniscus": 2, "medial_meniscus": 3}'
kneemesh-mesh --breps '{"medial_meniscus": "medial_meniscus.brep"}' --meniscus.max-size 1.0

The last command writes medial_meniscus_tet4.msh. Each run saves the configuration it used, so it can be repeated with --config. Without --config it is saved as settings<timestamp>.json in the working directory. With --config and overrides, the changed configuration is saved beside the original as <name>_modified.json. Section overrides change only the fields given, leaving the section’s other values from --config.

For a hexahedral mesh, build the menisci’s B-reps with --meniscus.hex-blocks true, and preferably the cartilage’s with --cartilage.hex-blocks true (--femoral-cartilage.hex-blocks true for the femoral cartilage), and mesh them with --element-type hex8, hex20, or hex27. --element-type applies to every structure but the bones; --element-types gives individual structures their own, such as --element-types '{"medial_meniscus": "hex8", "patellar_cartilage": "tet4"}', and each structure’s output suffix takes its own type. Bones take theirs from their own sections (see Bones). --meniscus.hex-design picks the meniscus’s hexahedra. butterfly, the default, builds five blocks meshed with structured hexahedra, whose regular layout makes the more simulation-ready mesh; their edges around the middle section and along the meniscus take the two sizes below. swept_quads lofts the meniscus with planar caps; meshing fills its largest cross-section with unstructured quadrilaterals (gmsh’s Frontal-Delaunay for quads) about --meniscus.transverse-size across, 1 mm by default, and sweeps them along the meniscus in layers about --meniscus.circumferential-size apart, 2 mm by default, so the elements shrink across the narrower sections toward the horns. For swept_quads, the section’s thin inner edge is first cut off straight where the section is a quarter of its mean outer height thick (--meniscus.inner-clip-ratio, 0.25 by default for swept_quads, or --meniscus.inner-clip-height in mm): the loft gets a narrow face for the cut, whose top and bottom are edges the hexahedra keep nodes on, in place of an edge too sharp for them to follow. On the real menisci the cut is about 1.1-1.2 mm tall and costs 0.5-1.5% of the volume. --meniscus.inner-clip-ratio 0 keeps the sharp edge. Either way the slice contours put each other corner of the section between the same two points on every slice, so the B-rep’s lines along the meniscus follow its ridges. On the real menisci at the default sizes the swept hexahedra of the clipped loft optimize to minimum scaled Jacobians of about 0.5, means of 0.92-0.93, and largest Frobenius aspect ratios of 7.2-8.7, against 0.38-0.41 unclipped and the butterfly’s 0.27-0.29 with twice the elements. Mesh optimization moves the nodes afterward for better scaled Jacobians with optimize_hexahedral_mesh(). The meniscus optimizes by default, since swept hexahedra can start with a few inverted elements, weighs aspect ratios lightly (--meniscus.aspect-weight 0.02), since the scaled Jacobian alone stretched elements near the horns to aspect ratios of 90, and flattens warped surface faces (--meniscus.warp-weight 50, tuned for the butterfly blocks; swept hexahedra were tuned at 30). A face’s warp is the angle between the two triangles a diagonal splits it into; scaled Jacobians and aspect ratios miss it, though it is what makes a surface look crumpled, so the log reports it after each optimization too. On the real menisci the warp term lowers the butterfly’s 99th percentile of warp from 20-33 degrees to 17-23, and halves the swept hexahedra’s, to 16-27 degrees; the cartilage takes --cartilage.optimize true. It first untangles any inverted elements. Boundary nodes may then leave the B-rep’s surface by up to --meniscus.surface-deviation times the local element size, 0.1 by default, without changing the volume overall; --meniscus.surface-deviation 0 keeps them on its tangent planes.

Segmentations¶

When the structures are segmented in separate files, assemble them once with kneemesh-assemble, a pipeline configured by LabelMapAssembly:

kneemesh-assemble --label-maps '{"lateral_meniscus": "lateral.nii", "medial_meniscus": "medial.nii"}' --output knee.nrrd

It writes knee.nrrd and its labels in knee_labels.json beside it, logging to assemble<timestamp>.log. Structures get the values 1, 2, … in the order given.

kneemesh-brep reads a segmentation and builds every structure named in it. Give the segmentation one of two ways:

  • A multilabel LabelMap with --label-map, and --labels naming its values as JSON, such as '{"lateral_meniscus": 2, "medial_meniscus": 3}'. --labels is required, and values it does not name are ignored. An assembled LabelMap is read with --label-map knee.nrrd --labels "$(cat knee_labels.json)".

  • Single-label LabelMaps with --label-maps, assembled within the run as kneemesh-assemble does, one file per structure by name, such as '{"lateral_meniscus": "lateral.nii", "medial_meniscus": "medial.nii"}'. Every nonzero voxel of a file is its structure. assemble_label_maps() resamples them onto one grid, in the first file’s orientation, with the smallest voxel spacing along each axis and a background margin of --padding voxels. --interpolator chooses label_gaussian (the default) or nearest_neighbor. Where structures overlap after resampling, the one listed later wins. --assembled-label-map knee.nrrd also writes the assembled LabelMap and knee_labels.json, as kneemesh-assemble does. read_segmentation() reads them in Python.

Every structure in the segmentation is built. A cartilage needs the bone beneath it in the segmentation too: patella for patellar_cartilage, femur for femoral_cartilage, and tibia for either tibial cartilage. The bone tells the cartilage’s sides apart, and its surface is built as well.

{
  "label_maps": {"lateral_meniscus": "lateral_meniscus.nii", "medial_meniscus": "medial_meniscus.nii"},
  "assembled_label_map": "knee.nrrd",
  "output": "_hex.brep",
  "meniscus": {"hex_blocks": true}
}

The repository’s examples/ holds configurations for the real structures in tests/dat, run from the repository root. The knee_* ones handle all nine structures in one go:

kneemesh-assemble --config examples/knee_assemble.json   # writes examples/knee.nrrd
kneemesh-brep --config examples/knee_brep_hex.json       # or knee_brep_tet.json
kneemesh-mesh --config examples/knee_mesh_hex8.json      # or knee_mesh_tet4.json

Cartilage¶

The tibial and patellar cartilage are built as sheets between their articular side and the side against their bone. Each B-rep is one solid: the two sides, and a straight wall around the perimeter, tilted so it meets the two sides at about equal angles, where a wall along the sheet’s normal met the articular side, which curves down at the rim, at up to 130 degrees or more. Either element shape can mesh it, but the cartilage settings pick defaults by hex_blocks. For hexahedra, the perimeter curves no more tightly than a 3 mm radius, so elements can follow the real lateral tibial cartilage’s notch, and regions thinner than 0.2 of the mean thickness are thickened by lowering the bone side. For tetrahedra, the perimeter follows the label more closely, with a 1 mm radius, regions thinner than 0.1 of the mean are thickened, and those thinner than 0.05 of the mean, or missing from the label, become holes. Thickened cartilage may overlap its bone.

kneemesh-brep --label-maps '{"tibia": "tibia.nii", "lateral_tibial_cartilage": "lateral.nii"}' --cartilage.hex-blocks true --output _hex.brep
kneemesh-mesh --breps '{"lateral_tibial_cartilage": "lateral_tibial_cartilage_hex.brep"}' --element-type hex8 --cartilage.optimize true

Hexahedral meshing partitions the sheet itself. It meshes one side with quadrilaterals about --cartilage.element-size across, 2 mm by default, and stacks --cartilage.elements-through hexahedra, 3 by default, along straight columns to the other side; the columns through the walls follow the wall. --cartilage.quad-algorithm picks gmsh’s algorithm for the quadrilaterals: quasi_structured, the default, aligns them with a cross field, leaving few irregular nodes, the more simulation-ready layout, but sizes down to 0.3 of the target on curved sheets; frontal_delaunay keeps their size closest to the target and optimizes to slightly better scaled Jacobians, but leaves many irregular nodes. If gmsh leaves triangles, the size is changed by a few percent until it does not. --cartilage.save-partition true also writes <structure>_partition.brep, the blocks the quadrilaterals’ irregular nodes divide the sheet into, one volume each: about 10 to 30 with quasi_structured, and mostly single elements with frontal_delaunay.

Optimizing cartilage hexahedra also weighs their Frobenius aspect ratios, by --cartilage.aspect-weight, 0.5 by default: optimizing the scaled Jacobian alone squares up corners by stretching elements, to a largest aspect ratio of 8 on the real patellar cartilage against 2.3 at 0.5. Optimizing keeps each column of nodes through the walls straight. The thinnest stretches of the rim hold the worst elements; --cartilage.perimeter-inset cuts them off at the cost of area.

Femoral cartilage¶

The femoral cartilage wraps the condyles, so it is built as a sheet over the cylinder its side against the femur wraps, unrolled into a U: the trochlea joining the two condyles around the intercondylar notch. Its femoral_cartilage settings extend the cartilage settings, with the same defaults by hex_blocks, and its solid and hexahedra are made the same way.

kneemesh-brep --label-maps '{"femur": "femur.nii", "femoral_cartilage": "femoral.nii"}' --femoral-cartilage.hex-blocks true --output _hex.brep
kneemesh-mesh --breps '{"femoral_cartilage": "femoral_cartilage_hex.brep"}' --element-type hex8 --femoral-cartilage.optimize true

Tetrahedra default to --femoral-cartilage.element-size too, since the default from the oriented bounding box, about 30 mm across for the wrapped cartilage, gives nearly flat tetrahedra.

Bones¶

The femur, tibia, and patella are not meshed with hexahedra, and their geometry is not a B-rep. Each bone has its own settings section, femur, tibia, or patella, so options are given as --tibia.shaft-cut-fraction 0.3; below, --<bone> stands for any of them. kneemesh-brep writes each bone’s closed, triangulated surface as .stl:

  1. bone_mask() fills the label’s cavities. Where the scan cuts a shaft off, as it does the femur’s and tibia’s, --<bone>.shaft-cut-fraction removes that fraction of the bone’s length along the shaft from the cut end, square in the voxel map, to make the mesh smaller; 0, the default, keeps the shaft as scanned. A shaft is cut off where the label’s last slice along an image axis is a whole cross-section, nearly the same as the slices 2 mm inside it.

  2. The surface is the isocontour of the mask’s smoothed distance field, as for the other structures, smoothed by a windowed sinc filter with feature-edge smoothing off and coordinates normalized. Smoothing rounds the flat end of a cut shaft.

  3. decimate_surface() reduces the triangles to --<bone>.triangle-size, 1 by default, which moves the real bones’ surfaces by at most 0.02. gmsh took minutes to reparametrize the real femur’s 340,000 triangles.

kneemesh-mesh meshes a bone given its .stl in --breps. gmsh splits the surface into patches it can map to a plane and meshes them (kneemesh.mesh.bone). --<bone>.element-type chooses the elements, separately from --element-type:

  • tri3 (the default) or tri6 triangles, or quad4, quad8, or quad9 quadrilaterals, on the surface, about --<bone>.element-size across, 2 by default. Quadrilateral meshes hold quadrilaterals only. The mesh is smoothed across the seams between gmsh’s patches, and every node, second-order ones included, lies on the surface.

  • tet4 or tet10 tetrahedra through the volume, --<bone>.cortical-element-size across, 1 by default, within --<bone>.cortical-distance of the surface, 3 by default, where the cortical bone lies. Deeper, in the cancellous bone, their edges grow by --<bone>.cancellous-grad per unit of depth, 0.5 by default, up to --<bone>.max-size, 4 by default; a smaller grad makes a smoother transition and more elements.

kneemesh-brep --label-maps '{"femur": "femur.nii", "tibia": "tibia.nii"}' --femur.shaft-cut-fraction 0.4 --tibia.shaft-cut-fraction 0.3
kneemesh-mesh --breps '{"femur": "femur.stl"}' --femur.element-type quad4
kneemesh-mesh --breps '{"femur": "femur.stl"}' --femur.element-type tet10 --femur.cortical-distance 2

Node and element sets¶

Every mesh has named sets of faces on its surface, element sets, and of their nodes, node sets, by the same names:

Structure

Sets

Every structure

surface: every boundary face, or a bone’s surface mesh’s own elements

Bones

surface only

Cartilage

articular_side, the side away from the bone, and bone_side, the side against it; the walls between them are in surface only

Menisci

anterior_horn and posterior_horn, the caps at its ends, and body, the rest

A face is an element of its own, a triangle or quadrilateral of the volume elements’ order, numbered after the volume elements. The anterior horn is the one toward the patient’s front, at lower y in the LPS physical space SimpleITK reads images into, so it relies on the image’s orientation being right.

.vtu and .vtkhdf hold the sets; gmsh’s formats hold only the elements. In a .vtu, the faces are cells after the volume elements, and each set is a point or cell array, 1 for its members. A .vtkhdf is a multiblock: an elements grid of the volume elements, and surfaces and node_sets blocks holding a grid per set, of its faces or of vertices at its nodes. In an assembled .vtkhdf the set blocks are named <structure>_<set>, as in a .vtu, and every set grid’s node_id is the same node, at the same coordinates, as in its structure’s elements grid. Every node and element carries its number, from 1, as node_id and element_id.

--assembled-mesh also writes every structure’s mesh as one, as .vtu or .vtkhdf. Nodes and elements are numbered on through the structures in the order of --breps, each structure’s volume elements before its faces, and sets are named <structure>_<set>, such as lateral_meniscus_anterior_horn. The .vtu is one grid with a structure cell array indexing the field array structures; the .vtkhdf has a block per structure, named for it. Structures share no nodes.

kneemesh-mesh --config examples/knee_mesh_tet4.json --assembled-mesh knee.vtkhdf

Element quality¶

.vtu and .vtkhdf meshes also hold each element’s quality as cell arrays, by element_quality(): the scaled Jacobian, the Frobenius aspect ratio, the edge ratio (longest corner edge over shortest, comparable across element shapes), each tetrahedron’s smallest dihedral angle in degrees, which sees slivers the scaled Jacobian and edge ratio miss, and each quadrilateral face’s warp. A metric that does not apply to an element, such as the dihedral angle of a hexahedron, is NaN there, so a mixed mesh carries every metric of any of its shapes. Metrics are measured on corner nodes, except that the scaled Jacobian and aspect ratio of high-order hexahedra and tetrahedra take the element’s own Jacobian at its corner nodes and at the points of the Gauss rules a simulation integrates it with: curved edges can invert an element whose straight-sided corners are valid, and leave one valid whose corners are not. Hexahedral optimization keeps those points valid too, and second-order tetrahedra are curved by gmsh’s high-order optimization (Mesh.HighOrderOptimize 2), which untangles the thin ones beside the surface. The log reports each structure’s worst and mean values as it is meshed.

scaled_jacobian

warp
